Indian lithium-ion battery packs: an export plan you can test

The product

A 5 kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) pack, 48 V, about 40 kg, for home and telecom backup and light electric vehicles. Cells are imported without duty under Advance Authorisation, then built into packs in Tamil Nadu with an Indian battery management system.

Not in scope: bare cells (India makes about 1 GWh, too little to export) and phone batteries (sold by the hundred million at under $5 a unit).

HS 8507.60 mixes phone cells, EV packs and storage racks. A unit value of $3 and one of $780 can sit in the same code. Every price comparison below says which unit it uses.

But one niche is working: Japan

India's exports rose from $1.3 m (2016) to $138 m (2023), then fell to $60 m as a German buyer disappeared ($76 m to $1.7 m). Japan kept growing and now takes 43% of India's exports, at $52/kg against Japan's $27/kg average. Japan's own import data shows $25 m from India, matching India's $25.9 m (mirror check).

Source: Trade Map time series 2016-2025, and Japan import data by supplier, 2025.

Who are the rivals? Export similarity by destination

ESI uses the Session 4 formula, the sum of the smaller of two export shares, with destination markets as the categories because we compare one product. A high score means India and the rival chase the same buyers.

China (28.8), Viet Nam (28.4) and Korea (21.8) overlap most. In Japan, China holds 81% and Korea 9%, while India is sixth with 0.8%. We cannot beat China on cost. We can be the second source Japanese buyers are told to find, with Indian-built safety electronics and traceable cells.
